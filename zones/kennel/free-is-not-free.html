Free Is Not Free

  • Goal: you will name what a “free” model offer costs you, sort offers into four kinds, and decide which data may go to which.
  • Before you start: Adopt the Pet (a small local model, “the Pet”) and The Locked Cabinet.
  • Time: about 20 minutes. The hands-on step uses a dummy prompt and no sensitive data.

The Pet is free because it runs on your machine. Many other “free” offers run on someone else’s machine, so someone is paying. Sometimes it is the provider, who gains your attention or your data. Sometimes it is you, in limits and in time. Using any of them is optional.

Free tiers change often: providers have ended them, shrunk them or turned them into short trials. Read the provider’s own page before you rely on one, and treat every number on it as a snapshot.

1. Four kinds of free

Kind Who runs it What you give up
Open weights, run by you Your laptop or cluster (the Pet) Speed and quality; some memory
Free tier of a provider The model’s maker or a hosting company Rate limits; sometimes your prompts
Router or gateway A middleman forwarding to other providers A second party in the path; a free list that churns
Reseller of unknown origin Unknown Everything you send

If a site offers a closed frontier model with no card and no limits, ask who pays for it. Send such a site nothing you would not post publicly.

2. What to check on any free offer

  1. Free tier or trial? A trial ends, and a free tier may too. Several of OpenCode Zen’s free models are free for a limited time.
  2. What are the limits? Look for requests per day and per minute, and what happens past them. On Cloudflare Workers AI, past the daily allowance further operations fail with an error.
  3. Is a card or other detail needed? Some free tiers need a payment method, a phone number or a data opt-in.
  4. Does the free tier train on your prompts? This matters most.
    • Google AI Studio: on unpaid services Google uses submitted content to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. Paid services differ.
    • Groq: it does not retain inference data by default, and may keep logs for up to 30 days.
    • OpenRouter: it does not log prompts by default, and retention varies with the provider behind the model.
  5. Who else is in the path? A router adds a party. Read its terms and the terms of the provider it picks. One router can send you to providers with different rules.

The terms can differ per model inside one service. OpenCode Zen lists some free models with zero retention and others that may use your data for training. Read the line for the model you picked.

Sensitive or unpublished data does not go to a hosted free tier. Use the Pet, or an approved service.

3. A worked comparison: Mistral’s terms

Mistral’s terms show how much one provider’s rules can vary. Read each point as a question to ask of any provider.

  • Training use depends on product and plan. Mistral Vibe (its terminal coding agent) Free, Pro, Teams and Student have training on, with an opt-out. Vibe Enterprise is opted out by default. Mistral Studio’s free mode may use data for training.
  • Opt-outs are per product. For Studio and the API, the opt-out is in the Admin Panel under Privacy, and opting out of one product does not opt you out of another. For Labs and Public Preview models, the opt-out does not apply.
  • Retention has its own options. Zero data retention exists for some API requests on pay-as-you-go, and each request is reviewed. Agents, Conversations, Files, Chat and Vibe are excluded. You do not need zero retention to opt out of model training. For retention periods, check Mistral’s current terms.
  • Where inference runs can be chosen. The global API endpoint does not commit to an inference location. The EU endpoint (api.eu.mistral.ai) runs in EU and EFTA data centers, so choose it on purpose if you need a European location. If location matters for Le Chat or Vibe, ask the vendor.
  • A local open-weight model keeps prompts on your machine. Open-weight Mistral models (for example ministral-3:8b, Apache 2.0) can run locally through Ollama. Tool or web calls may still send data out.

The sensitive-data rule holds for every plan above: unpublished or sensitive data stays on the Pet or an approved service.

4. Pairing a free model with an agent

Free endpoints often speak an OpenAI-compatible protocol (a common way of sending chat requests). OpenCode can connect to an OpenAI-compatible gateway; its Providers page in the Lore below shows how. Free models differ in how well they follow tool instructions, so test one on a small task before you trust it.

5. Try one, safely

Pick one free tier from a provider you have read about. Create the key in the provider’s console and keep it out of chat and out of files in your project. Then make one call with a harmless prompt. The shape below is the common OpenAI-compatible one; take the real base URL and model name from the provider’s page.

export FREE_API_KEY="..."          # set it in your shell, not in a file you commit
curl -sS "$BASE_URL/chat/completions" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $FREE_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"model": "MODEL_NAME", "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": "Say hello in one sentence."}]}'

Then fill in one row:

Provider Kind Limit per day Card needed? Trains on prompts? Who else sees them? Date checked

If you cannot fill a cell from the provider’s own page, write “not stated”. That is a finding too.
