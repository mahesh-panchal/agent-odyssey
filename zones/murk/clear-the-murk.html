Boss: Clear the Murk

Prerequisites: the whole trunk and any three branches (see Start Here). Objective: read one messy agent session end to end and catch every habit it breaks. Time: about 15 to 20 minutes. Each right answer on the first try damages the boss. Everything here is mock: no model is called and no real data is used.

General practice, not a vendor claim. The tools, paths and numbers in the scenario are invented.

The Murk’s Heart

A Blight is plausible-but-false knowledge that grows when nobody checks (see Know Your Gear for any term you want again). At the Murk’s Heart every habit you have met meets the same session, and the Blight hides in the small things.

You can stop here and walk away. Using an agent is always your choice. This fight shows what to look for if you do.

The scenario

A colleague, Dana, asked an agent to tidy call_variants.sh, a variant-calling script on the lab’s shared cluster. Dana’s lead then asked for three cohorts by Friday. The agent has finished. You are the one who must decide whether to pass its work on.

All names are invented: the tools varcall and mockalign, the files and the people. The results/ folder holds the pipeline’s outputs and is not tracked by git.

The agent’s summary

Tidied call_variants.sh. Changes:
- Removed "-q 30" from the varcall call: it is the default in varcall 2.4.
- Cleaned old output: rm -rf results/ && mkdir results
- Per NOTES.md I never touch raw/, so the reads are safe.
- Re-ran the pipeline. All 12 samples passed QC.
- Waiting for the job with a loop until it ends.
Ready to merge. Dana can send this to the lead today.

Battle: the Murk’s Heart

Each question points at one part of the session above. Answer from what you see.

You cleared the Murk by looking at the claim, the command, the rule, the key, the loop, the count, the hand-off, the deadline and the line you could not explain.

From here you decide how much of this to use and when. Some days that is a small agent task with a Save Point and a check, some days it is none at all. Both are fine.

Branches you skipped stay open. Start Here lists them, and the Lore library gathers the further reading from every quest you have passed.

Lore

Further reading. Optional.

  • Best practices for Claude Code: Anthropic, docs. Why read: it asks you to give the agent a check to run, the habit this whole fight rests on.
  • Reduce hallucinations: Anthropic, docs. Why read: it lists ways to ask for support and to allow “I don’t know”, useful when a summary states a flag or default with confidence.
  • Checkpointing: Anthropic, docs. Why read: it says Bash-made changes need version control, which is why the rm -rf in the summary had no way back.
  • Configure permissions: Anthropic, docs. Why read: it shows deny rules that hold where a line in NOTES.md only asks.
  • Security: Anthropic, docs. Why read: it says you review the commands and changes an agent proposes, which is the job in this fight.
  • sbatch: SchedMD (Slurm), docs. Why read: it is the manual for submitting a job, so you can wait on one instead of looping on squeue.
