The Locked Cabinet

Prerequisites: Grow the Carapace. Objective: keep keys, settings and sensitive data out of the agent’s reach, and know what changes on a shared cluster. Time: about 15 minutes. General practice, not a vendor claim. Your own data agreement and cluster policy override anything here.

Much research data is not sensitive. Some of it is, and then special agreements apply. This quest uses no real data and practises habits, so that the careful choice becomes automatic.

Three ideas

  1. A prompt may leave your machine. With a hosted model, anything the agent reads may be sent to the provider. A model running locally (the Pet, via Ollama) keeps prompts on your machine, though web or tool calls it makes may not. Whether either is allowed for your data is decided by your agreement.
  2. Secrets stay out of content. API keys, tokens, SSH keys and passwords belong in a secret store, kept out of the project folder, and out of chats and any instructions file you commit. Be aware that an environment variable is visible to any command the agent runs, so a read rule alone is not a seal.
  3. A shared cluster is shared. What is fine on your laptop can disturb other people on a login node, or break a data policy on shared storage. Cluster rules apply to agents exactly as they apply to you.
Note

A deny rule on your secrets files (see Grow the Carapace) stops the agent’s file-reading tool. An agent that can run shell commands may still be able to read them another way, so also keep secrets out of the project folder, and use a sandbox or shell rules where you can. Not confirmed here: exactly how each tool treats shell reads of denied files.

Unpublished ideas and results

A common worry: my unpublished results or ideas will leak and someone will get there first. Another version is a company showing a proof of something you spent years on. This page does not claim either has happened. The habits below lower the chance and the cost of being wrong.

What the docs say (Claude Code data usage page, read 2026-11-17):

  • Training use means a provider may learn from your prompts to improve future models. For Free, Pro, Max and Ultra accounts it depends on a privacy setting that you can change. For Team, Enterprise and API accounts, Anthropic says it does not train on code or prompts sent to Claude Code under commercial terms, unless the customer opts in.
  • Retention for consumer accounts is 5 years if you allow model improvement and 30 days if you do not. Commercial accounts have 30 days as standard, and zero data retention for qualified Enterprise accounts.
  • Not checked: the claude.ai chat privacy pages and the full terms, and any other vendor’s tools. Read the terms of the exact tool and plan you use.

Habits:

  1. Keep unpublished ideas, results and sequences out of consumer chat tools unless the terms say how your text is used and you accept that.
  2. Prefer an agreement your institution has reviewed. Ask your data steward or research office what is approved. They can give advice on contracts and ownership. This quest gives none.
  3. Consider a local model. Ollama says it does not see your prompts or data when you run locally (see Adopt the Pet). Tools and web calls the model makes may still send data out.
  4. Date-stamp your work. A preprint, a repository tag or a dated lab notebook entry records when you had it.
  5. Share on a need-to-know basis. Send collaborators what they need for the task.

Honour-system side quest

Nobody checks this and nothing depends on it. It is here because the real rules are in a document your group holds, and this site cannot see it.

Find the data-handling agreement or policy that applies to your project (or ask your group leader where it is). Read the passages about where data may be processed and whether it may be sent to external services, and write down in your own notes what they say.
List where your own API keys and settings files live (for example a .env file or a shell profile), and check that your agent’s permission rules deny reading them.
